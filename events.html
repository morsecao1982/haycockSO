Life Science · Core Knowledge

Anatomy & Physiology

2 students 50 min Written test / lab stations

Participants are assessed on their understanding of the anatomy and physiology of the Respiratory, Digestive, and Immune Systems of the human body.

What to bring: 1 sheet of paper (both sides), 2 Class II calculators
  • Respiratory: spirograms, gas exchange, COPD, asthma, emphysema
  • Digestive: alimentary canal, chemical digestion, ulcers, hepatitis
  • Immune: innate vs. adaptive immunity, HIV/AIDS, autoimmune diseases, hypersensitivities

Life Science · Core Knowledge

Botany

2 students 50 min Written test / stations

Participants demonstrate their knowledge of plant life and general botany principles. Supervisors may provide live/preserved specimens and microscopes.

What to bring: 1 sheet of paper (both sides), 2 Class II calculators
  • Plant cell structure, roots, stems, leaves, flowers
  • Photosynthesis, plant genetics & reproduction
  • Major plant groups (monocot/dicot, embryophytes, algae)
  • Plant diseases, paleo-botany, GMOs, nutrient cycles

Life Science · Core Knowledge

Disease Detectives

2 students 50 min Written test

Participants use investigative epidemiology skills to study disease, injury, health, and disability in populations. In partnership with the CDC.

What to bring: 1 sheet of paper (both sides), 2 Class II calculators
  • Background & surveillance: epidemiology history, natural history of disease
  • Outbreak investigation: epi curves, case definitions, attack rates, relative risk
  • Patterns, control & prevention: incidence/prevalence, disease control strategies

Life Science · Core Knowledge

Heredity

2 students 50 min Written test / stations

Teams answer questions, solve problems, and analyze data pertaining to classic and molecular genetics. Emphasis on quantitative reasoning.

What to bring: 1 sheet of paper (both sides), 2 Class II calculators
  • Mendelian genetics: Punnett squares, pedigrees, probability
  • Mitosis & meiosis; karyotype analysis
  • DNA structure, replication, transcription & translation
  • Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium; genetic drift
  • PCR techniques

Life Science · Hybrid

Water Quality

2 students 50 min Eye Protection C Test + hands-on

Participants are assessed on their understanding of marine and estuary aquatic environments, including coral reef ecology and water monitoring.

What to bring: 1 sheet of paper (both sides), 2 Class II calculators, student-built salinometer
  • Marine & estuary ecology (30%): aquatic food webs, estuaries, harmful algal blooms
  • Coral reef organisms (30%): ID of coral, fish, invertebrates by common name
  • Water monitoring (30%): salinity, pH, dissolved oxygen, turbidity data interpretation
  • Salinometer testing (10%): measure 1–10% NaCl concentration with self-built device

Earth & Space · Core Knowledge

Dynamic Planet

2 students 50 min Test / lab stations

Teams complete tasks related to Earth's fresh waters: rivers, groundwater, lakes, wetlands, and the hydrologic cycle. Partnership with NOAA.

What to bring: 1 binder (any size), 2 Class II calculators
  • Hydrologic cycle: water budgets, precipitation, runoff, infiltration
  • Drainage basins, stream order, channel types (braided, meandering)
  • Fluvial geomorphology: floodplains, oxbow lakes, deltas, alluvial fans
  • Groundwater: aquifers, karst features, sinkholes, springs
  • Lakes & wetlands: stratification, formation, bogs vs. marshes
  • Topographic map interpretation; stream gauge hydrographs

Earth & Space · Core Knowledge

Meteorology

2 students 50 min Written test / stations

Participants analyze Severe Weather & Storms: forecasting data, radar imagery, and the science behind tornadoes, hurricanes, and extreme precipitation. Partnership with NOAA.

What to bring: 1 three-ring binder, 2 Class II calculators, U.S. map with state names
  • Observation tools: Doppler radar, radiosondes, satellite imagery (visible/IR/water vapor)
  • Severe thunderstorms: single-cell, multicell, supercell, MCC structure
  • Tornadoes: EF scale, TVS, hook echo; waterspouts
  • Hurricanes: life cycles, Saffir-Simpson scale, storm surge, ENSO effects
  • Winter storms: blizzards, nor'easters, lake-effect snow, ice storms
  • Historic events: Katrina, Sandy, Ida, Ian, Helene; April 2011 tornadoes

Earth & Space · Core Knowledge

Remote Sensing

2 students 50 min Test / stations

Participants demonstrate understanding of remote sensing principles and interpret imagery from Landsat, Sentinel, MODIS, GOES, and SRTM satellites to observe Earth. Sponsored by IEEE GRSS.

What to bring: 1 three-ring binder, 2 Class II calculators, 2 protractors, 2 rulers
  • Electromagnetic spectrum, reflection, scattering; Wien's Law, Stefan-Boltzmann Law
  • Passive sensors (optical, thermal) & active sensors (radar, SAR, altimetry)
  • Satellite orbit types: LEO, MEO, geostationary, polar
  • Image interpretation: natural/false color, thermal, radar, elevation, land cover
  • Earth observation: vegetation, geohazards (volcanoes, landslides, floods), glacial movement
  • Scale, distance, area measurements from imagery

Earth & Space · Core Knowledge

Rocks & Minerals

2 students 50 min Station identification

Teams identify rocks and minerals from the official 2027 National List and demonstrate knowledge of geologic processes, classification, and economic uses.

What to bring: 1 binder, 1 commercial field guide (annotated OK), 1 magnifying glass, 1 Class II calculator, copy of 2027 National R&M List
  • Mineral properties: hardness, luster, streak, cleavage, specific gravity
  • Mineral families: silicates, carbonates, sulfides, oxides, native elements, etc.
  • Igneous rocks: textures (aphanitic, porphyritic, vesicular), felsic vs. mafic
  • Sedimentary rocks: clastic/chemical/biochemical; grain size; depositional environments
  • Metamorphic rocks: foliated vs. non-foliated; index minerals; protoliths
  • Bowen's Reaction Series; Rock Cycle; economic uses

Earth & Space · Core Knowledge

Solar System

2 students 50 min Written test

Participants demonstrate knowledge of habitability within and beyond the Solar System — conditions, geology, chemistry, and biology relevant to life.

What to bring: 2 sheets of paper (both sides) — no calculators allowed
  • Focus objects: Venus, Mars, Ganymede, Europa, Enceladus, Titan, Makemake, Bennu, 67P/Churyumov–Gerasimenko
  • Extrasolar systems: TRAPPIST-1, Kepler-452, LHS 1140
  • Habitability conditions: temperature, atmosphere, liquid water, tidal heating
  • Extremophiles; building blocks of life (proteins, nucleic acids, lipids)
  • Exoplanet detection: transits, radial velocity, microlensing, direct imaging
  • Spacecraft: Europa Clipper, Cassini-Huygens, Perseverance, JWST, Dragonfly, OSIRIS-REx

Physical Science · Hybrid

Circuit Lab

2 students 50 min Written test + hands-on

Participants complete hands-on tasks and answer questions about electricity and electronics. Written test is 50–75% of the score; hands-on is 25–50%. Supported by Kinder Morgan Foundation.

What to bring: Unlimited binder/notes, 2 Class III calculators, optional personal multimeter
  • DC/AC circuits: Ohm's Law, series/parallel, Kirchhoff's Laws (States+)
  • Digital logic: gates, truth tables, boolean expressions, De Morgan's Law, adders
  • Diodes, LEDs, PN junctions
  • Hands-on: breadboard circuits, mystery resistors, troubleshooting, 3-way switches
  • Historical: Ampere, Coulomb, Kirchhoff, Volta, Ohm, Hertz, Boole

Chemistry · Lab

Crime Busters

2 students 50 min Eye Protection C + apron Hands-on lab

Given a crime scenario, students test evidence using chemistry to identify the perpetrator. 50% qualitative analysis, 25% scene analysis/write-up.

What to bring: 1 sheet of paper each, 1 Class II calculator each, goggles, apron, Div-B Chemistry Lab Equipment List items
  • Qualitative analysis of unknown solids (NaCl, flour, cornstarch, baking soda, sand, etc.) and liquids
  • Polymer testing: hair (human/dog/cat), fibers (animal/vegetable/synthetic), recyclable plastics (PETE, HDPE, PS, PP, PVC)
  • Paper chromatography: Rf values
  • Fingerprints (whorls/loops/arches), DNA electropherograms, shoeprints, soil, spatter patterns
  • Written analysis explaining which evidence implicates which suspect

Chemistry · Hybrid

Food Science

2 students 50 min Eye Protection C + apron Impound: notebook Lab + notebook

Teams study dairy science and experiment with milk products. A pre-tournament notebook of experiments is impounded and graded. At Regionals, 1 lab task is completed on-site.

What to bring: 1 sheet of paper each, 2 Class III calculators, Chem Lab Equipment List items, 1-pint & 1-gallon Ziploc bags, cheesecloth, 500–600 mL container w/ lid
  • Pre-tournament: Notebook with 3+ experiments per task (9+ total), using the Food Science Experiment Form
  • Lab Task 1 — Ice Cream Formation: combine milk/cream, sugar, vanilla; freeze using ice + salt Ziploc method; maximize height
  • Lab Task 2 — Curd Mass: add acid to milk; drain through cheesecloth; measure curd mass percentage
  • Lab Task 3 — Butter: agitate cream until butter forms; measure yield

Physical Science · Hybrid

Thermodynamics

2 students 50 min Impound: device Written test + device

Teams build a device prior to the tournament to collect heat from a lamp and raise the temperature of water. Score = written test (50%) + temperature gain (30%) + prediction accuracy (20%).

What to bring: Unlimited binder/notes, 2 Class III calculators, pre-built device (fits 30×30×30 cm cube)
  • Zeroth, 1st & 2nd Laws of thermodynamics; thermodynamic cycles & engines
  • Heat transfer types: conduction, convection, radiation
  • Phase transitions, latent heat, specific heat capacity, ideal gas law
  • Historical: Kelvin, Black, Joule, Carnot, Planck, Clausius, Boltzmann, Maxwell
  • Device: no thermostats, vacuum-sealed containers, or hazardous materials (asbestos, fiberglass); must accept a 9 oz plastic cup of water; ES provides 100W heat lamp

Boomilever

2 students 10 min Eye Protection B Build event

Build a cantilevered wood structure that extends from a Testing Wall and holds maximum sand load. Score = Load Supported ÷ Structure Mass. Supported by Cleveland-Cliffs Foundation.

What to bring: Pre-built structure only (no tools, no notes — ES provides all apparatus)
  • Structure must be wood + adhesive only — no plywood, bamboo, paper, or bark
  • Attaches to Testing Wall via Mounting Bolt; load arm must extend 40–45 cm horizontally
  • Load Scored Bonus: hold ≥15 kg and touch wall only above 15 cm line (+5,000 g)
  • ES loads sand into bucket; loading stops when structure fails
  • Tiebreaker: lowest structure mass → closest estimated load to actual load

Elastic Launched Gliders

2 students 10 min (6-min flight period) Eye Protection B Build event

Build elastic-launched gliders to achieve maximum time aloft. Up to 5 official flights; best 3 scores count. Sponsored by the National Free Flight Society.

What to bring: Up to 2 gliders, launch handle(s), measurement box(es), flight log
  • Glider must fit in team-provided box: 32.0 × 24.0 × 47.0 cm max
  • Mass 3.5–10.0 g; no Boron filaments; no pre-glued kits
  • Flight log with 10+ test flights (3 required parameters + 1 extra): +20% bonus multiplier
  • Score = sum of 3 best Flight Times × bonus multiplier

Build

Hovercraft

2 students 8 min competition Eye Protection B Impound Build event

Build a self-propelled air-levitated vehicle to carry nickels down a 185 cm track as close as possible to a Target Time (6–18 seconds). Score = Distance + Time + Mass.

What to bring: Pre-built vehicle (impounded), 2 Class III calculators, tools
  • Vehicle must fit 40 × 40 × 40 cm box when levitating; must have ¼" wooden dowel at front
  • Only batteries, wires, switches, resistors, capacitors, and up to 2 motors — no ICs
  • Batteries: max 12V, no lithium or lead; all propellers must be shielded
  • Up to 2 runs; best run score is Final Score
  • Optional ramp available but halves Distance Score and gives Mass Score of 0

Ping-Pong Parachute

2 students 5 min (2 launches) Eye Protection B Build event

Build bottle rockets to launch a ping pong ball + parachute for maximum time aloft. Best single-launch time wins. Event is held indoors.

What to bring: Up to 2 rockets, 2 ping pong balls, 2 parachutes, optional manual bicycle pump with gauge
  • 1-liter plastic carbonated beverage bottle only; no glue on pressure vessel, tape only
  • No metal anywhere; no water; air pressure only for propulsion
  • Practice log with 10+ flights (3 required parameters + 2 extra): required to choose your own pressure; without it, capped at safe ES-determined pressure & score ×0.70
  • Ceiling hit penalty: ×0.25; parachute fails to separate: ×0.90

Build

Roller Coaster

2 students 12 min (8-min run time) Eye Protection B Impound Build event

Build a track to guide a ball using gravity alone to match a Target Time of 30–60 seconds. Score = Size + Time + optional Elevator & Loop bonuses.

What to bring: Pre-built coaster + ball(s), tools, spare parts (all impounded)
  • Max size: 30 × 80 × 60 cm; ball ≥1 cm diameter, always visible
  • Gravity-only propulsion; no springs, rubber bands, magnets, or electronics
  • Time Bonus: +5 pts/sec up to Target Time; Time Penalty: −5 pts/sec past Target Time
  • Elevator bonus: +50 pts each for Type 1 (1st-class lever + wheel & axle) and Type 2 (2nd-class lever + pulley) if ball rises ≥10 cm
  • Loop bonus: +3 pts per cm of loop height if ball completes it while unsupported at top

Scrambler

2 students 12 min (8-min event time) Eye Protection B Impound Build event · Low score wins

A falling mass (≤2 kg) propels a vehicle carrying an egg to stop as close as possible to a Terminal Barrier (7–10 m away) without breaking the egg. Sponsored by International Motors.

What to bring: Pre-built Scrambler + spare parts (impounded); tools; Class III calculator
  • Entire Scrambler must fit in a 100 × 50 × 100 cm box; falling mass ≤2 kg; cannot touch floor
  • Backstop: solid unpadded 5×5 cm wood (no balsa); egg rests on two ¼"–⅜" dowels
  • Vehicle started by removing a #2 pencil provided by ES; stopping mechanism must be automatic
  • Score = 100 + (Distance from wall × 2 pt/cm) + Run Time + Can Bonus; lower is better
  • 2 runs allowed; best (lower) score counts; broken egg ends competition
  • Commercial kit allowed if team makes 2+ documented functional modifications

Inquiry · Core Knowledge

Codebusters

Up to 3 students 50 min Decryption exam

Teams decrypt encrypted messages using historical and modern cryptanalysis techniques. Score = Exam + Timing Bonus (first Aristocrat) + Special Bonus (hardest ciphers).

What to bring: Writing utensils only — no resource materials. ES provides Morse code, letter frequency, and cipher tables
  • Aristocrat & Patristocrat (monoalphabetic substitution, K1 alphabet)
  • Baconian Cipher; Atbash; Caesar (shift) Cipher
  • Fractionated Morse; Porta Cipher; Nihilist Cipher
  • Complete Columnar Transposition (≤9 columns); 5×5 Checkerboard; Homophonic Cipher
  • Xenocrypt (Spanish + K1 English alphabet); Cryptarithms (base-10 math)
  • Timing bonus for 1st question: 2 × (600 − seconds)

Inquiry · Lab

Experimental Design

Up to 3 students 50 min Eye Protection C On-site experiment

Teams design, conduct, and report the findings of an experiment entirely on-site using unknown materials. First 20 minutes: design & conduct. Last 30 minutes: analyze & write up.

What to bring: Goggles, 1 timepiece, 1 ruler, 1 Class II calculator — none may be used as part of the experiment
  • Materials identity unknown until start; ES provides identical sets for all teams
  • Must identify independent variable (IV), dependent variable (DV), and 3+ controlled variables (CVs)
  • Scored on 51-pt Part I (design) + 55-pt Part II (data, graph, CER analysis, conclusion)
  • Off-topic: ×0.75 penalty; fake/no data: ×0.25 penalty; no clean-up: ×0.95

Write It Do It

Exactly 2 students 50 min (25 write + 20 build) Communication challenge

One student sees an object and writes a description of how to build it (no drawings). The other student reads only the description and attempts to build the object. Scored on how close the replica is to the original.

What to bring: Writer brings writing utensil only — no other resources allowed for either participant
  • Object is built from materials like K'nex, Lego, straws, Popsicle sticks, etc. — unknown until event
  • Writer has 25 min; NO drawings or diagrams allowed (disqualification); words, numerals, symbols OK
  • Builder has 20 min with written description only; cannot see original object
  • Each piece scored on: size, color, location, orientation, and connection
  • Drawing a subsection = Tier 2; drawing the full model = disqualification
